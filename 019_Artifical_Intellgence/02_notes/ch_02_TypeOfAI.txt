2.1 CLASSIFICATION OF ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE
================================================================

Artificial Intelligence can be classified in different ways.

Two traditional classification approaches are:

1. Classification by Capability
2. Classification by Functionality

Other useful classifications describe the intended purpose,
degree of autonomy, and role of humans in an AI system.

IMPORTANT:
These classifications describe different dimensions.

For example, an AI system may simultaneously be:

- Narrow AI by capability.
- Limited-memory-like in its use of past information.
- Generative AI by its output.
- Partially autonomous in its operation.
- Human-in-the-loop when deployed in a high-risk workflow.

Therefore, these categories are not necessarily mutually
exclusive.


----------------------------------------------------------------
A. CLASSIFICATION BY CAPABILITY
----------------------------------------------------------------

This classification describes the breadth of tasks an AI
system can perform.

The three commonly discussed categories are:

    1. Artificial Narrow Intelligence (ANI)
    2. Artificial General Intelligence (AGI)
    3. Artificial Super Intelligence (ASI)


                    ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE
                              |
                +-------------+-------------+
                |             |             |
               ANI           AGI           ASI
                |             |             |
             Narrow         General        Beyond
             tasks          capability     human-level
                                            capability

ANI: Existing practical AI systems generally operate within
     particular task boundaries, even when they support
     many related tasks.

AGI: A proposed capability level involving broad, flexible
     intelligence across many different domains.

ASI: A hypothetical capability level substantially exceeding
     human intellectual performance across most relevant domains.

2.2 ARTIFICIAL NARROW INTELLIGENCE (ANI)
================================================================

DEFINITION
----------

Artificial Narrow Intelligence (ANI) refers to AI designed
or trained to perform a particular task or a bounded collection
of related tasks.

It is also commonly called:

    Narrow AI
    Weak AI

However, "Weak AI" is a philosophical classification that is
often associated with narrow AI; the terms do not have to be
identical in every discussion.


SIMPLE EXPLANATION
------------------

Imagine a computer system that can play chess better than
most people.

It can:

- Analyze chess positions.
- Search possible moves.
- Evaluate board positions.
- Select a strong move.

However, being excellent at chess does not automatically
enable it to:

- Diagnose a disease.
- Write a business contract.
- Drive a car.
- Recognize every object in an image.
- Manage a company.

Its chess capability does not automatically transfer to
unrelated tasks.


IMPORTANT CHARACTERISTICS OF ANI
--------------------------------

1. TASK-SPECIFIC CAPABILITY

   The system is designed or trained for a defined task
   or a bounded set of tasks.

2. LIMITED TRANSFER

   Knowledge or capability learned in one setting may not
   transfer reliably to a very different setting.

3. DEFINED OPERATING CONDITIONS

   The system may depend on specific inputs, interfaces,
   tools, or environmental assumptions.

4. MEASURABLE PERFORMANCE

   Its performance can often be evaluated using task-specific
   metrics.

5. POSSIBLE HIGH PERFORMANCE

   Narrow systems can outperform humans in selected tasks.

6. NO REQUIREMENT FOR HUMAN-LIKE CONSCIOUSNESS

   A system can perform a narrow task successfully without
   demonstrating consciousness or human-like experience.


REAL-WORLD EXAMPLES OF ANI
--------------------------

EXAMPLE 1: SPAM CLASSIFIER

Input:
    An email message.

Task:
    Classify the message as spam or not spam.

Output:
    Predicted category.

It does not automatically become an expert in unrelated fields.


EXAMPLE 2: CHESS ENGINE

Input:
    A chessboard position.

Task:
    Select a strong chess move.

Output:
    Recommended move.

The system specializes in chess.


EXAMPLE 3: FACE RECOGNITION SYSTEM

Input:
    A face image.

Task:
    Compare or identify a face under specified conditions.

Output:
    Similarity score or identity-related result.

Such systems can have serious privacy and fairness implications.
Their accuracy also depends on data and operating conditions.


EXAMPLE 4: PRODUCT RECOMMENDATION SYSTEM

Input:
    User activity and product information.

Task:
    Rank potentially relevant products.

Output:
    Recommended products.

The system specializes in recommendation.


EXAMPLE 5: SPEECH RECOGNITION

Input:
    Audio containing speech.

Task:
    Convert speech into text.

Output:
    A transcript.

Speech recognition does not automatically imply the ability
to perform every task involving language.


EXAMPLE 6: HOUSE-PRICE PREDICTION

Input:
    House characteristics.

Task:
    Estimate the selling price.

Output:
    Predicted price.

This is a specialized prediction task.


ADVANTAGES OF ANI
-----------------

1. Can perform specialized tasks efficiently.
2. Can achieve high accuracy under suitable conditions.
3. Can automate repetitive or data-intensive work.
4. Can be evaluated using measurable criteria.
5. Can be integrated into larger applications.
6. Can reduce the cost of particular workflows.


LIMITATIONS OF ANI
------------------

1. May fail on unfamiliar inputs.
2. May depend on specific training data.
3. May not transfer well to unrelated tasks.
4. Can inherit bias from its data.
5. Can produce incorrect predictions.
6. May require retraining or redesign when conditions change.


IS ANI THE MOST COMMON FORM OF AI TODAY?
----------------------------------------

Yes. Most deployed AI systems are specialized in their design,
training, operating conditions, or practical capabilities.

Examples include fraud detectors, image classifiers,
recommendation systems, and speech recognition systems.

Modern foundation models complicate this distinction because
a single model can perform many different tasks.

Nevertheless, broad task coverage alone does not prove that
a model possesses fully general intelligence.


EXAM DEFINITION
---------------

Artificial Narrow Intelligence is an AI capability category
in which a system performs a particular task or a bounded
collection of tasks without necessarily possessing the broad,
flexible intelligence associated with humans.

2.3 ARTIFICIAL GENERAL INTELLIGENCE (AGI)
================================================================

DEFINITION
----------

Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) refers to a proposed
form of AI capable of learning, reasoning, and solving problems
across a broad range of domains with flexibility comparable
to human general intelligence.

There is no universally accepted operational definition of AGI.

Different researchers and organizations use different criteria
to describe generality, autonomy, reliability, and capability.


SIMPLE EXPLANATION
------------------

Imagine an AI system that can:

1. Learn mathematics.
2. Understand scientific concepts.
3. Learn a new programming language.
4. Plan a journey.
5. Adapt to an unfamiliar game.
6. Learn from relatively limited experience.
7. Transfer knowledge between different domains.
8. Solve new problems without being separately engineered
   for every individual task.

A system exhibiting sufficiently broad and reliable abilities
of this kind would be closer to the general-intelligence goal.

This is a conceptual illustration, not a universally agreed
test for AGI.


IMPORTANT CHARACTERISTICS ASSOCIATED WITH AGI
----------------------------------------------

1. GENERALIZATION

   Applying knowledge to new situations.

2. FLEXIBLE LEARNING

   Learning new skills without requiring a completely separate
   system for every task.

3. TRANSFER LEARNING

   Reusing knowledge acquired in one domain to help solve
   problems in another domain.

4. REASONING

   Using available information to derive conclusions.

5. PLANNING

   Organizing actions to achieve goals.

6. ADAPTATION

   Responding effectively to changing circumstances.

7. BROAD TASK COMPETENCE

   Performing well across many different intellectual tasks.

8. ROBUSTNESS

   Maintaining reliable performance across varied conditions.

9. AUTONOMY

   Depending on the definition, operating independently
   for extended periods may be an important criterion.

IMPORTANT:
Autonomy is not sufficient for AGI. A narrow system can
operate autonomously while remaining specialized.


HYPOTHETICAL EXAMPLE OF AGI
---------------------------

Suppose an AI system is placed in a new environment.

Initially, it has no specific training for the environment.

It receives instructions and can:

- Observe its surroundings.
- Learn how the environment works.
- Develop a strategy.
- Test its strategy.
- Learn from failure.
- Apply relevant knowledge from other domains.
- Complete unfamiliar tasks reliably.

If these capabilities extended broadly across important
domains, they would contribute evidence toward AGI.

However, success in a single demonstration would not establish
that a system meets all possible AGI definitions.


WHY IS AGI DIFFICULT?
---------------------

Building AGI is difficult because intelligence involves
many interconnected abilities.


CHALLENGE 1: GENERALIZATION
---------------------------

A model may perform well on familiar examples but fail
when the environment changes.

AGI would require broad and robust transfer to new situations.


CHALLENGE 2: COMMON-SENSE REASONING
-----------------------------------

Humans use extensive background knowledge about physical
objects, social situations, cause and effect, and everyday life.

AI systems can possess substantial learned knowledge yet
still make surprising common-sense mistakes.


CHALLENGE 3: LONG-TERM PLANNING
------------------------------

A complex objective may require hundreds or thousands
of dependent actions.

An error early in the process can affect everything that
follows.

A generally capable system would need reliable planning,
monitoring, and recovery mechanisms.


CHALLENGE 4: CONTINUAL LEARNING
-------------------------------

A system may need to acquire new skills without losing
previously learned capabilities.

This problem is related to continual learning and catastrophic
forgetting.


CHALLENGE 5: CAUSAL REASONING
-----------------------------

Recognizing statistical relationships is not always enough.

A system may need to distinguish correlation from causation
and predict what happens when conditions are changed.


CHALLENGE 6: ROBUSTNESS
-----------------------

A generally capable system must handle unfamiliar inputs,
ambiguous instructions, and unexpected conditions.


CHALLENGE 7: EVALUATION
-----------------------

How do we determine whether a system is genuinely general?

A reliable evaluation framework must distinguish:

- Memorization from generalization.
- Short demonstrations from sustained capability.
- Fluent answers from correct answers.
- Benchmark success from real-world competence.
- Narrow expertise from broad competence.


CHALLENGE 8: SAFETY AND CONTROL
-------------------------------

A powerful system may affect important resources or decisions.

Developers must consider whether its actions remain within
appropriate constraints and whether failures can be detected
and corrected.


IS AGI THE SAME AS HUMAN CONSCIOUSNESS?
---------------------------------------

No.

AGI concerns the breadth and flexibility of capabilities.

Consciousness concerns questions about subjective experience
and awareness.

A system could potentially satisfy a behavioral definition
of general intelligence without establishing that it has
subjective experiences.

The relationship between intelligence and consciousness
remains a subject of scientific and philosophical debate.


IS TODAY'S AI AGI?
------------------

There is no universally accepted test that settles the question
for all modern AI systems.

Modern general-purpose AI models can perform many tasks,
including language processing, coding assistance, image
analysis, and some forms of reasoning.

However, they may still show limitations in:

- Reliability
- Long-horizon planning
- Consistent reasoning
- Adaptation to unfamiliar environments
- Autonomous operation
- Grounding in the physical world

Whether a particular system qualifies as AGI depends partly
on the definition and evaluation criteria being used.

It is important not to treat broad capability as automatic
proof of fully general intelligence.


EXAM DEFINITION
---------------

Artificial General Intelligence is a proposed category of AI
characterized by broad, flexible intellectual capabilities
across many domains rather than specialization in a narrow
task. Its precise definition and achievement criteria remain
contested.


================================================================
2.4 ARTIFICIAL SUPER INTELLIGENCE (ASI)
================================================================

DEFINITION
----------

Artificial Super Intelligence (ASI) is a hypothetical form
of AI whose intellectual capabilities substantially exceed
those of humans across most or nearly all relevant domains.


SIMPLE EXPLANATION
------------------

Imagine a future system that greatly exceeds the strongest
human experts in:

- Scientific research
- Mathematical problem-solving
- Strategic planning
- Engineering
- Learning efficiency
- Complex analysis
- Developing new technologies

If such superiority were broad and sustained, the system
might fit a proposed definition of ASI.


IMPORTANT CHARACTERISTICS
-------------------------

1. SUPERHUMAN PERFORMANCE

   Performance substantially exceeds human capability across
   a broad range of relevant intellectual tasks.

2. ADVANCED PROBLEM-SOLVING

   The system could solve problems that are extremely difficult
   for human researchers.

3. RAPID KNOWLEDGE PROCESSING

   It might analyze large amounts of information quickly,
   depending on its hardware and design.

4. ADVANCED PLANNING

   It might plan complex activities across long time horizons.

5. POTENTIAL FOR SCIENTIFIC DISCOVERY

   It could potentially accelerate research and engineering.

6. MAJOR SAFETY CONCERNS

   If its capabilities and autonomy were extremely high,
   controlling harmful behavior could become especially important.


HYPOTHETICAL EXAMPLE
--------------------

Suppose a future AI system can:

1. Develop new scientific theories.
2. Design and test advanced materials.
3. Discover highly effective medicines.
4. Solve complex engineering problems.
5. Improve its problem-solving methods.
6. Outperform the best human specialists across many domains.

This hypothetical system might be considered superintelligent
if its superiority were sufficiently broad and substantial.

This is a thought experiment, not a description of an existing
system demonstrated to meet such criteria.


POTENTIAL BENEFITS
------------------

If appropriately controlled and successfully applied, a
superintelligent system could potentially contribute to:

- Scientific discoveries
- Medical research
- Energy optimization
- Environmental modeling
- Engineering innovation
- Mathematical research
- Improved decision support


POTENTIAL RISKS
---------------

1. LOSS OF HUMAN CONTROL

   A system's actions might become difficult to supervise.

2. GOAL MISALIGNMENT

   Its behavior might not match the intentions or values
   of the people responsible for it.

3. MISUSE

   Powerful capabilities could be used for harmful purposes.

4. UNINTENDED CONSEQUENCES

   Actions taken to achieve an objective might create
   harmful side effects.

5. CONCENTRATION OF POWER

   Control over highly capable systems could become
   concentrated in a small number of organizations.

6. SECURITY RISKS

   Unauthorized access or misuse could create serious harm.


IMPORTANT DISTINCTION
---------------------

ASI is hypothetical.

Claims about its precise capabilities, timeline, or social
effects involve substantial uncertainty.

It should not be presented as an existing technology whose
superhuman general capabilities have already been established.


EXAM DEFINITION
---------------

Artificial Super Intelligence is a hypothetical form of AI
that substantially surpasses human intellectual performance
across a broad range of domains.

2.7 REACTIVE MACHINES
================================================================

DEFINITION
----------

Reactive Machines are AI systems described as selecting
responses to the current situation without using a stored
history of past observations as part of their decision process.


SIMPLE EXPLANATION
------------------

A reactive system receives the current input and produces
a response according to its programmed or learned policy.

Simplified representation:

    Current Input
         |
         v
    Decision Process
         |
         v
       Action


EXAMPLE: SIMPLE GAME-PLAYING SYSTEM
-----------------------------------

Suppose a system evaluates the current board position and
selects a move.

In a simplified reactive design, it does not update a persistent
memory of previous game experiences between decisions.

Its behavior depends on the current board position and its
fixed or learned decision mechanism.

This is a conceptual example. Actual game-playing systems
can use search, internal state, or stored information.


IBM DEEP BLUE
-------------

IBM's Deep Blue chess system is commonly used in introductory
discussions as an example of reactive AI.

Deep Blue evaluated chess positions and searched possible
moves using specialized hardware and chess algorithms.

Its move selection depended heavily on the current position
and search results.

However, describing Deep Blue as a reactive machine is a
simplification of its architecture. It used search and
maintained internal computational state during analysis.


CHARACTERISTICS
---------------

1. Focuses on the current situation.
2. Does not necessarily learn from past interactions.
3. Can perform effectively in structured environments.
4. May use fixed rules, search, or a learned policy.
5. Does not need a human-like memory of personal experiences.


ADVANTAGES
----------

- Can be effective in clearly defined environments.
- May be relatively simple to evaluate.
- Can make consistent decisions under similar conditions.
- Does not necessarily require a persistent experience history.


LIMITATIONS
-----------

- Cannot necessarily use past experiences to improve.
- May struggle when important information is not currently
  observable.
- May not adapt to changing conditions without redesign
  or an updated policy.


EXAM DEFINITION
---------------

A reactive machine is an AI system that selects actions
based primarily on the current situation without relying
on a stored history of past observations in its decision
policy.


================================================================
2.8 LIMITED MEMORY AI
================================================================

DEFINITION
----------

Limited Memory AI refers to systems that use information
from previous observations, historical data, or temporary
internal state to support current predictions or decisions.


SIMPLE EXPLANATION
------------------

Unlike a purely reactive system, a limited-memory system
can use relevant information from the past.

For example, a vehicle's driving system may consider:

- Its current speed.
- The previous measured speed.
- The estimated movement of nearby vehicles.
- Recent camera observations.
- The recent positions of pedestrians.

This information can help estimate how the environment
is changing.


EXAMPLE 1: DRIVER-ASSISTANCE SYSTEM
-----------------------------------

A system monitors the vehicle's surroundings.

At time T1:
    A nearby vehicle is 30 meters ahead.

At time T2:
    The nearby vehicle is 25 meters ahead.

At time T3:
    The nearby vehicle is 20 meters ahead.

The system can use these observations to estimate whether
the distance is decreasing and how quickly.

It may then issue a warning or support braking decisions.

This is a simplified example of using temporal information.
Actual vehicle systems use more complex sensor processing,
prediction, control, and safety mechanisms.


EXAMPLE 2: DEMAND FORECASTING
-----------------------------

An AI model receives historical sales data.

Input:
    Sales from previous weeks or months.

Task:
    Predict future demand.

Output:
    Estimated sales for the next period.

The system uses past observations to produce a prediction.


EXAMPLE 3: SPEECH RECOGNITION
-----------------------------

Speech recognition systems process sequences of audio
information.

Their models can use contextual information from surrounding
audio frames or tokens to interpret speech.

However, not every speech recognition system uses persistent
long-term memory. Sequence context and long-term memory
are not identical concepts.


EXAMPLE 4: CONVERSATIONAL AI WITH HISTORY
-----------------------------------------

A conversational system receives:

    User:
        My preferred programming language is Python.

Later:

    User:
        Show me an example of a loop.

If the system retains and uses the earlier message, it may
respond with a Python example.

This is an example of using conversation history.

Whether the system retains information across separate
sessions depends on its memory design and configuration.


TYPES OF MEMORY-LIKE INFORMATION
-------------------------------

A system may use:

1. RECENT OBSERVATIONS
   Information from the latest sensor readings.

2. TEMPORARY INTERNAL STATE
   Information maintained while processing a task.

3. CONVERSATION HISTORY
   Earlier messages in a conversation.

4. HISTORICAL DATA
   Records used for training or prediction.

5. EXTERNAL STORED INFORMATION
   Information retrieved from a database or another system.


IMPORTANT DISTINCTION
---------------------

Training data is not automatically the same as an AI system's
runtime memory.

A model may be trained on historical data and then operate
without remembering each new interaction.

Likewise, a system may retrieve information from a database
without permanently changing its learned model parameters.


ADVANTAGES
----------

- Can use context from previous observations.
- Can support predictions about changing environments.
- Can maintain relevant information during a task.
- Can improve decision-making when history is informative.


LIMITATIONS
-----------

- Historical information may be incomplete.
- Old information may become irrelevant.
- Stored context may contain errors.
- Processing and storing history can increase costs.
- A model can misuse or misunderstand previous observations.


EXAM DEFINITION
---------------

Limited Memory AI uses relevant past observations, historical
information, or internal state to support current decisions
or predictions.


================================================================
2.9 THEORY OF MIND AI
================================================================

DEFINITION
----------

Theory of Mind AI refers to a proposed category of AI that
would model and reason about the beliefs, intentions, goals,
knowledge, or emotional states of other agents.

In psychology, Theory of Mind refers to the ability to
understand that other individuals can have beliefs,
perspectives, intentions, and knowledge different from
one's own.


SIMPLE EXPLANATION
------------------

Imagine a robot working with a human.

The human says:

    "I cannot reach that box."

A system with sophisticated social reasoning might infer:

- The human wants the box.
- The box is outside the human's reach.
- Assistance may be useful.
- The robot may need to move the box closer.

This requires more than simply detecting the spoken sentence.

The system must interpret the human's likely goal and the
relevant circumstances.


EXAMPLE: COLLABORATIVE ROBOT
----------------------------

A human and robot work together in a warehouse.

The robot observes that the human is waiting near a shelf.

It combines information about:

- The task they are performing.
- The human's recent actions.
- The objects involved.
- The instructions already given.
- The human's likely goal.

The robot predicts that the human needs a particular item
and offers assistance.

A system with sufficiently sophisticated models of other
agents' mental states would illustrate the Theory of Mind idea.

This example is hypothetical and does not establish that
the system possesses genuine human-like social understanding.


IMPORTANT CHARACTERISTICS
-------------------------

A proposed Theory of Mind system might:

1. Model other agents' beliefs.
2. Estimate intentions.
3. Reason about goals.
4. Track what another person knows.
5. Predict behavior based on beliefs and goals.
6. Interpret social context.
7. Adjust communication based on the situation.


BELIEF VS FACT
--------------

Suppose Alice believes that a book is in Room A.

Unknown to Alice, the book has been moved to Room B.

The actual state is:

    Book location = Room B

Alice's belief is:

    Book location = Room A

A system capable of representing Alice's belief separately
from reality could reason about what Alice is likely to do.

This distinction is important in social reasoning.


IS EMOTION DETECTION THE SAME AS THEORY OF MIND?
-----------------------------------------------

No.

An AI system might classify a facial expression or infer
that a voice sounds upset.

This is not sufficient to establish that it understands
another person's beliefs, intentions, or perspective.

Social reasoning involves more than recognizing emotional
patterns.


CHALLENGES
----------

1. Human intentions are not directly observable.
2. The same behavior can have different explanations.
3. People may have incomplete or incorrect beliefs.
4. Cultural and situational context can change interpretation.
5. Emotional expressions do not always reveal actual feelings.
6. Incorrect inferences can lead to inappropriate actions.


CURRENT STATUS
--------------

Theory of Mind AI is best understood as a conceptual category
and research goal.

Modern systems can model some aspects of beliefs, intentions,
social situations, and human behavior.

However, these capabilities do not establish that a system
has achieved a complete human-like Theory of Mind.

Performance must be evaluated for specific tasks and conditions.


EXAM DEFINITION
---------------

Theory of Mind AI is a proposed form of AI capable of modeling
and reasoning about the beliefs, intentions, knowledge, and
other mental states of other agents.


================================================================
2.10 SELF-AWARE AI
================================================================

DEFINITION
----------

Self-Aware AI refers to a hypothetical form of AI that would
possess genuine self-awareness or an internal subjective
understanding of itself.


SIMPLE EXPLANATION
------------------

Imagine a system that not only describes its capabilities
but also possesses a genuine subjective awareness of its
own existence and internal experiences.

Such a system would go beyond merely generating statements
about itself.

This is a hypothetical concept. There is no established
scientific test demonstrating that modern AI systems possess
subjective consciousness.


SELF-DESCRIPTION VS SELF-AWARENESS
----------------------------------

Consider the following statement from an AI assistant:

    "I am an AI model designed to answer questions."

This statement may be generated from information about
the system's design.

It does not prove that the system has subjective awareness.

Similarly, a robot may monitor:

- Its battery level.
- Its internal temperature.
- Its sensor status.
- Its motor condition.

These capabilities constitute system monitoring.

They do not, by themselves, establish consciousness.


POSSIBLE FEATURES OF HYPOTHETICAL SELF-AWARE AI
-----------------------------------------------

1. Self-representation.
2. Awareness of internal state.
3. A persistent model of its own capabilities.
4. Recognition of its limitations.
5. Monitoring of its own actions.
6. Potentially, subjective experience.

The first five features can be implemented to varying degrees
in computational systems.

The sixth feature raises a separate and unresolved question.


IMPORTANT DISTINCTIONS
----------------------

SELF-MONITORING:
    Tracking internal variables or system status.

SELF-MODELING:
    Maintaining a representation of one's own capabilities
    or state.

SELF-REPORTING:
    Producing statements about oneself.

SELF-AWARENESS:
    A broader and disputed concept that may involve subjective
    awareness of one's own existence or experience.


EXAMPLE: ROBOT BATTERY MONITORING
---------------------------------

A robot checks its battery level.

If the battery falls below a threshold, it returns to a
charging station.

This demonstrates state monitoring and goal-directed control.

It does not establish subjective self-awareness.


IS SELF-AWARE AI CURRENTLY A REALITY?
-------------------------------------

There is no established evidence that current AI systems
possess subjective consciousness in the human sense.

Systems can produce sophisticated self-descriptions and
monitor aspects of their own operation.

Those abilities should not automatically be interpreted
as evidence of conscious experience.


WHY IS THIS TOPIC DIFFICULT?
----------------------------

1. Consciousness lacks a universally accepted scientific
   explanation.

2. Observable behavior may not settle questions about
   subjective experience.

3. Self-reports from AI systems are not reliable proof
   of consciousness.

4. Different philosophical theories imply different criteria.

5. There is no universally accepted test for machine
   consciousness.


EXAM DEFINITION
---------------

Self-Aware AI is a hypothetical category of AI that would
possess genuine awareness of its own existence or subjective
internal experience. It should not be confused with ordinary
system monitoring or self-description.

Status as a          Existing       Common         Research goal   Hypothetical
category             design idea    design pattern

Consciousness        Not implied    Not implied    Not implied     Part of the
                                                                  hypothetical
                                                                  concept


IMPORTANT EXAM NOTE
-------------------

The traditional four-category framework is useful for
introductory study.

Modern AI systems do not always fit neatly into these boxes.

For example, a modern language model may use conversation
history and reason about a person's likely intentions, while
still not possessing subjective awareness.

Therefore, use this classification as a conceptual framework,
not as a complete description of every modern AI architecture.


================================================================
2.12 WEAK AI VS STRONG AI
================================================================

The terms Weak AI and Strong AI are commonly used to discuss
the scope and nature of machine intelligence.

They are related to ANI and AGI, but they are not always
perfectly interchangeable.


WEAK AI
-------

DEFINITION:

Weak AI refers to AI designed to perform particular tasks
or provide specific computational capabilities without
claiming that it possesses human-like general intelligence
or consciousness.

EXAMPLES:

- Spam detection
- Chess engines
- Image classification
- Recommendation systems
- Speech recognition


CHARACTERISTICS:

1. Often task-focused.
2. May achieve excellent performance in its domain.
3. Does not imply consciousness.
4. Can use rules, machine learning, or deep learning.
5. Can operate autonomously within limited boundaries.


STRONG AI
---------

The term Strong AI has more than one usage.

In many introductory courses, it is used for the idea of
AI with general, human-like intellectual capabilities.

In philosophy, Strong AI can refer to a stronger claim:
that a suitably designed computer system could genuinely
possess a mind or understanding, rather than merely simulate
intelligent behavior.

These meanings should not be treated as identical.


EXAMPLE OF THE GENERAL-INTELLIGENCE USAGE
-----------------------------------------

Imagine a system that can learn and solve many unfamiliar
tasks across science, mathematics, communication, and planning
with broad flexibility.

This would illustrate the general-intelligence interpretation
of Strong AI.

It would not, by itself, establish consciousness.


COMPARISON
----------

FEATURE                 WEAK AI                STRONG AI
----------------------------------------------------------------

Main idea               Specialized AI         Depending on usage:
                                               general intelligence
                                               or a stronger claim
                                               about genuine mind

Scope                   Bounded tasks          Potentially broad

Consciousness            Not implied            Philosophical versions
                                               may involve genuine
                                               understanding or mind

Current status          Widely deployed        Depends on definition;
                                               the stronger claims
                                               remain unresolved


RELATIONSHIP WITH ANI AND AGI
-----------------------------

A common introductory mapping is:

    Weak AI  ≈  ANI
    Strong AI ≈  AGI

But this is only an approximation.

ANI/AGI classify capability breadth.

Weak/Strong AI can also involve philosophical questions
about whether machine intelligence constitutes genuine
understanding or mind.


================================================================
2.13 SPECIALIZED AI
================================================================

DEFINITION
----------

Specialized AI is designed or configured to solve a particular
class of problems or perform a defined set of tasks.


EXAMPLES
--------

1. MEDICAL IMAGE AI

   Analyzes specified kinds of medical images.

2. FRAUD DETECTION AI

   Identifies suspicious financial transactions.

3. SEARCH RANKING AI

   Orders search results according to estimated relevance
   and other ranking objectives.

4. SPEECH RECOGNITION AI

   Converts audio into text.

5. INDUSTRIAL INSPECTION AI

   Identifies specified defects in manufactured products.

6. RECOMMENDATION AI

   Ranks items according to estimated user relevance.


ADVANTAGES
----------

- Easier to define a specific objective.
- Performance can be measured for a bounded task.
- Development can focus on a particular domain.
- Integration requirements can be clearly specified.


LIMITATIONS
-----------

- Limited transfer to unrelated tasks.
- May depend on domain-specific data.
- Can become unreliable when operating conditions change.
- May need substantial redesign to support new tasks.


SPECIALIZED AI VS ANI
---------------------

The terms overlap considerably.

ANI is a capability classification.

Specialized AI describes a system's purpose or scope.

In many real-world discussions, a specialized AI system
will also be considered narrow AI.


================================================================
2.14 GENERAL-PURPOSE AI
================================================================

DEFINITION
----------

General-purpose AI describes AI systems that can support
multiple different tasks rather than being restricted to
one specific task.

The term is used in several contexts, including research,
product development, and regulation.

Its exact meaning depends on the context.


SIMPLE EXPLANATION
------------------

Consider two systems.

SYSTEM A:
    Accepts a photograph and classifies it as a cat or dog.

SYSTEM B:
    Can answer questions, summarize documents, generate code,
    translate text, and analyze some images.

System B has broader task coverage.

This does not automatically establish that it has achieved AGI.


EXAMPLES OF GENERAL-PURPOSE CAPABILITIES
----------------------------------------

A general-purpose AI model may support:

1. Text generation.
2. Summarization.
3. Translation.
4. Question answering.
5. Coding assistance.
6. Document analysis.
7. Image understanding.
8. Structured information extraction.


IMPORTANT CHARACTERISTICS
-------------------------

1. Supports multiple tasks.
2. May accept different types of instructions.
3. Can be adapted to various applications.
4. May use tools or external information.
5. Can still have important capability limitations.


GENERAL-PURPOSE AI VS AGI
-------------------------

GENERAL-PURPOSE AI:

    Broad utility across multiple tasks.

AGI:

    A proposed level of broad, flexible, reliable intelligence
    across domains.

A model can be general-purpose without satisfying a demanding
definition of AGI.


EXAMPLE
-------

A language model can generate Python code and explain
mathematics.

However, it may still:

- Produce incorrect code.
- Make reasoning mistakes.
- Fail to execute a plan reliably.
- Need external tools to verify results.
- Require human supervision for important decisions.

Broad functionality is not equivalent to unlimited competence.


================================================================
2.15 AUTONOMOUS AI
================================================================

DEFINITION
----------

Autonomous AI refers to an AI system that can select or execute
actions with some degree of independence after receiving
an objective or entering an operating environment.


SIMPLE EXPLANATION
------------------

A basic AI assistant may answer:

    "Here are the steps to organize your files."

An autonomous file-management agent might:

1. Inspect a permitted directory.
2. Identify file categories.
3. Propose an organization plan.
4. Execute approved changes.
5. Verify the result.
6. Report any errors.

The second system performs actions rather than merely
describing what a person could do.


BASIC ARCHITECTURE
------------------

    User Goal
        |
        v
    AI System
        |
        v
    Observe Environment
        |
        v
    Plan / Select Action
        |
        v
    Execute Action
        |
        v
    Observe Result
        |
        +----------+
        |          |
        v          |
    Goal Achieved? |
        |          |
     Yes / No      |
        |          |
        +----------+
           Repeat if necessary


CHARACTERISTICS
---------------

1. Can select actions.
2. Can interact with an environment.
3. May use tools or APIs.
4. May operate over multiple steps.
5. May evaluate feedback.
6. Can be designed with varying levels of human oversight.


EXAMPLE 1: AUTONOMOUS ROBOT VACUUM
---------------------------------

A robot vacuum can:

- Map its surroundings.
- Detect obstacles.
- Plan a route.
- Clean selected areas.
- Return to its charging station.

Its autonomy is limited to its designed operating environment.


EXAMPLE 2: SOFTWARE OPERATIONS AGENT
------------------------------------

A software operations agent might:

- Inspect system metrics.
- Detect an alert.
- Gather diagnostic information.
- Recommend a response.
- Restart an approved service.
- Verify whether the issue is resolved.

High-impact actions should have appropriate permissions,
safeguards, and audit logs.


EXAMPLE 3: RESEARCH AGENT
-------------------------

A research agent may:

- Search approved sources.
- Retrieve documents.
- Extract information.
- Compare findings.
- Draft a report.
- Cite supporting sources.

The agent must still be evaluated for source quality,
incorrect conclusions, and fabricated citations.


AUTONOMY LEVELS
---------------

A simplified design spectrum is:

LEVEL 1: ASSISTIVE

    Provides recommendations.
    A human performs the action.

LEVEL 2: HUMAN-APPROVED EXECUTION

    Proposes an action.
    A human approves it.
    The system executes it.

LEVEL 3: BOUNDED AUTONOMY

    Executes predefined classes of actions independently
    within strict limits.

LEVEL 4: EXTENDED AUTONOMY

    Handles longer workflows with planning and feedback,
    while remaining subject to monitoring and constraints.


This is an illustrative spectrum, not a universal standard.


RISKS OF AUTONOMOUS AI
----------------------

1. Incorrect actions.
2. Misinterpretation of instructions.
3. Excessive tool permissions.
4. Unexpected side effects.
5. Repeated failure loops.
6. Inadequate verification.
7. Unauthorized access.
8. Difficulty reversing actions.


SAFETY MEASURES
---------------

- Least-privilege permissions.
- Human approval for high-impact actions.
- Action limits.
- Sandboxed execution where appropriate.
- Logging and monitoring.
- Verification after execution.
- Stop conditions.
- Rollback procedures.


IMPORTANT
---------

Autonomy is not the same as intelligence.

A system can be highly autonomous within a narrow domain.

For example, an industrial controller may operate independently
but still perform a limited set of predefined functions.


================================================================
2.16 HUMAN-IN-THE-LOOP AI
================================================================

DEFINITION
----------

Human-in-the-Loop AI (HITL) refers to an AI workflow in which
humans participate in one or more stages of data preparation,
training, evaluation, decision-making, or operation.


SIMPLE EXPLANATION
------------------

AI produces a recommendation.

A human reviews the recommendation before a consequential
decision is finalized.

This combines computational assistance with human judgment.


BASIC WORKFLOW
--------------

    Input Data
        |
        v
    AI Model
        |
        v
    Prediction / Recommendation
        |
        v
    Human Review
        |
        v
    Approved Decision
        |
        v
    Action


EXAMPLE 1: MEDICAL DECISION SUPPORT
----------------------------------

1. A model analyzes a medical image.
2. It highlights a potentially concerning region.
3. A qualified clinician reviews the image and output.
4. The clinician considers other relevant evidence.
5. The clinician makes the clinical decision.

The AI output supports the process but does not automatically
replace professional judgment.


EXAMPLE 2: CONTENT MODERATION
-----------------------------

1. An AI system flags a potentially harmful post.
2. A human moderator reviews ambiguous cases.
3. The moderator applies the relevant policy.
4. The system records the decision where appropriate.


EXAMPLE 3: AI-GENERATED CODE
----------------------------

1. An AI model suggests a code change.
2. A developer reviews the suggestion.
3. Automated tests are executed.
4. Security and correctness are checked.
5. The developer approves or rejects the change.


WHY HUMAN INVOLVEMENT MATTERS
-----------------------------

Humans can provide:

- Domain expertise.
- Contextual judgment.
- Ethical consideration.
- Error correction.
- Oversight.
- Accountability within the relevant organization.


LIMITATIONS OF HITL
-------------------

Human involvement does not automatically guarantee safety.

Potential problems include:

1. Reviewers may overlook mistakes.
2. Excessive alerts may cause fatigue.
3. Reviewers may trust AI too much.
4. Reviewers may lack necessary expertise.
5. Human review can increase cost and delay.
6. Poorly designed interfaces can hide important uncertainty.


HUMAN-IN-THE-LOOP VS HUMAN-ON-THE-LOOP
--------------------------------------

HUMAN-IN-THE-LOOP:

    A human actively participates in a decision or workflow,
    often approving or modifying an AI output before action.

HUMAN-ON-THE-LOOP:

    An AI system operates with human supervision.
    A human monitors behavior and may intervene when necessary.

HUMAN-OUT-OF-THE-LOOP:

    The system operates without routine human intervention
    in the relevant decision process.

These terms are used somewhat differently across industries.
The actual authority, intervention process, and safety
mechanisms matter more than the label alone.


EXAM DEFINITION
---------------

Human-in-the-Loop AI incorporates human participation into
the development, evaluation, or operation of an AI system
to provide oversight, judgment, correction, or approval.


================================================================
2.17 AI ASSISTANT VS AUTONOMOUS AI AGENT
================================================================

This distinction is important for modern AI applications.


AI ASSISTANT
------------

An AI assistant primarily helps a user by responding to
instructions, answering questions, or supporting tasks.

Example:

    User:
        Explain how to create a Python virtual environment.

    Assistant:
        Provides the explanation and commands.


AI AGENT
--------

An AI agent may use tools, planning, and feedback to pursue
an objective through multiple actions.

Example:

    User:
        Create a virtual environment and verify that Python
        and the required packages are available.

    Agent:
        1. Inspects the permitted environment.
        2. Selects appropriate commands.
        3. Executes the commands using an approved tool.
        4. Checks the results.
        5. Reports success or failure.


COMPARISON
----------

FEATURE              AI ASSISTANT           AI AGENT
----------------------------------------------------------------

Main role            Responds or helps      Pursues an objective
                                            through actions

Tool usage           Optional               Often important

Planning             May be limited         May plan multiple steps

Feedback loop        Not always             Common in agentic designs

Autonomy             Often lower             Can be higher

Risk                 Depends on use          Can increase with action
                                            permissions and autonomy


IMPORTANT
---------

The distinction is not absolute.

An assistant may use tools, and an agent may ask a human
for clarification.

The key question is whether the system merely provides
responses or also selects and executes actions to pursue
an objective.


================================================================
2.18 CURRENT STATE OF AI
================================================================

Modern AI has advanced considerably, but its capabilities
remain uneven across tasks and environments.


1. MACHINE LEARNING
-------------------

Machine learning is widely used in:

- Fraud detection.
- Recommendations.
- Forecasting.
- Classification.
- Search ranking.
- Predictive maintenance.


2. DEEP LEARNING
----------------

Deep learning is widely used in:

- Computer vision.
- Speech recognition.
- Natural language processing.
- Image analysis.
- Generative modeling.


3. GENERATIVE AI
----------------

Generative AI systems can produce:

- Text.
- Images.
- Audio.
- Video.
- Code.
- Other structured or unstructured content.


4. LARGE LANGUAGE MODELS
------------------------

LLMs can perform tasks such as:

- Answering questions.
- Summarizing documents.
- Generating text.
- Assisting with programming.
- Extracting structured information.
- Supporting selected reasoning tasks.


5. MULTIMODAL AI
----------------

Multimodal systems can process or generate combinations
of different data types, such as text, images, and audio.


6. AI AGENTS
------------

Agentic systems can combine models with:

- Tools.
- APIs.
- Planning.
- Memory.
- Feedback.
- Action execution.

Their reliability depends on the system design, task, and
available safeguards.


7. ROBOTICS
-----------

Robotics combines AI with physical systems that perceive
and act in the real world.

Examples include:

- Warehouse robots.
- Industrial robots.
- Autonomous navigation systems.
- Robotic inspection.


IMPORTANT LIMITATIONS OF MODERN AI
----------------------------------

Modern AI can still:

- Generate incorrect information.
- Make inconsistent predictions.
- Fail on unfamiliar conditions.
- Misinterpret ambiguous instructions.
- Use tools incorrectly.
- Produce insecure code.
- Reflect bias in data or design.
- Require significant human oversight.


CURRENT STATUS OF AGI
---------------------

Modern AI systems demonstrate broad and sometimes impressive
capabilities, but there is no universally accepted definition
or test that establishes AGI conclusively.

Claims about AGI should therefore specify the capability
criteria, evaluation methods, and limitations involved.

Broad usefulness, high benchmark scores, and fluent language
are not individually sufficient to settle the question.


================================================================
2.19 HOW TO IDENTIFY AN AI TYPE IN AN EXAM
================================================================

Use the following procedure when a question describes
an AI system.


STEP 1: CHECK THE TASK SCOPE
----------------------------

Question:
    Does the system perform a specialized task?

If yes:
    It may be classified as ANI or specialized AI.

Question:
    Does it demonstrate broad, flexible competence across
    many different domains?

If yes:
    The question may be referring to AGI, depending on
    the criteria provided.

Question:
    Does it substantially exceed human capability across
    most relevant intellectual domains?

If yes:
    The question may be describing hypothetical ASI.


STEP 2: CHECK HOW IT USES INFORMATION
-------------------------------------

Question:
    Does it rely primarily on the current situation?

Possible answer:
    Reactive machine.

Question:
    Does it use relevant historical observations or state?

Possible answer:
    Limited-memory AI.


STEP 3: CHECK THE MENTAL-STATE REQUIREMENT
------------------------------------------

Question:
    Does the system model another person's beliefs,
    intentions, or knowledge?

Possible answer:
    Theory of Mind AI.


STEP 4: CHECK THE SELF-AWARENESS CLAIM
--------------------------------------

Question:
    Does the description explicitly refer to hypothetical
    genuine self-awareness or subjective experience?

Possible answer:
    Self-Aware AI.

Remember:
    Monitoring a battery or describing its own design is
    not sufficient evidence of consciousness.


STEP 5: CHECK THE HUMAN ROLE
----------------------------

Question:
    Must a human review or approve the output?

Possible answer:
    Human-in-the-Loop AI.


STEP 6: CHECK THE LEVEL OF AUTONOMY
-----------------------------------

Question:
    Does the system independently select and execute actions?

Possible answer:
    Autonomous AI, depending on its degree of independence.


STEP 7: CHECK THE PURPOSE
-------------------------

Question:
    Is it designed for a particular domain?

Possible answer:
    Specialized AI.

Question:
    Does it support many different tasks?

Possible answer:
    General-Purpose AI.


IMPORTANT:
A single system may belong to several categories simultaneously.

2.20 PRACTICAL CASE STUDIES
================================================================

CASE STUDY 1: EMAIL SPAM FILTER
-------------------------------

System:
    Email spam classifier.

Characteristics:
    - Classifies messages.
    - Uses rules or a trained ML model.
    - May process large volumes of email.

Classification:
    - ANI.
    - Specialized AI.
    - Potentially limited-memory-like if it uses recent context,
      but ordinary classification does not require such memory.

Reason:
    Its core purpose is to classify email messages.


CASE STUDY 2: SELF-DRIVING VEHICLE
---------------------------------

System:
    An automated driving system.

Characteristics:
    - Uses sensors.
    - Detects roads and objects.
    - Estimates movement.
    - Plans trajectories.
    - Controls vehicle motion.

Possible classifications:
    - Narrow AI for the driving domain.
    - Uses historical observations or internal state.
    - Autonomous within its approved operating conditions.

Reason:
    It combines perception, prediction, planning, and action
    for a defined operational domain.

Important:
    The permitted operating conditions and degree of automation
    differ among systems. Not every driver-assistance feature
    is a fully autonomous driving system.


CASE STUDY 3: GENERAL-PURPOSE LANGUAGE MODEL
-------------------------------------------

System:
    A language model used for many tasks.

Characteristics:
    - Answers questions.
    - Summarizes documents.
    - Generates code.
    - Produces explanations.
    - May process images or other modalities.

Possible classifications:
    - General-purpose AI.
    - Generative AI.
    - Deep-learning-based AI if implemented using a deep
      neural network.
    - Narrow AI under definitions that reserve AGI for
      broader and more reliable general intelligence.

Reason:
    Broad task coverage does not automatically prove AGI.


CASE STUDY 4: MEDICAL DECISION SUPPORT
-------------------------------------

System:
    An AI model flags potentially concerning medical images.

Characteristics:
    - Processes medical images.
    - Produces a prediction.
    - A clinician reviews the result.

Possible classifications:
    - ANI.
    - Specialized AI.
    - Human-in-the-Loop AI.

Reason:
    The system performs a specialized task and incorporates
    human review into the workflow.


CASE STUDY 5: HYPOTHETICAL SUPERINTELLIGENCE
--------------------------------------------

System:
    A hypothetical AI consistently outperforms the strongest
    human experts across a very broad range of intellectual tasks.

Possible classification:
    ASI.

Reason:
    The defining hypothetical property is broad superhuman
    intellectual capability.

Important:
    This describes a conceptual scenario, not a claim that
    such a system has been established to exist.


================================================================
2.21 COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
================================================================

MISTAKE 1:
Treating ANI, AGI, and ASI as programming languages.

CORRECTION:
They are capability categories.


MISTAKE 2:
Claiming AGI and ASI are established everyday technologies.

CORRECTION:
AGI lacks a universally accepted achievement test, and ASI
is hypothetical.


MISTAKE 3:
Assuming every model that uses history is AGI.

CORRECTION:
Using historical information is a memory-related capability,
not proof of general intelligence.


MISTAKE 4:
Assuming a chatbot is self-aware because it says "I".

CORRECTION:
First-person language does not prove subjective experience.


MISTAKE 5:
Assuming an autonomous system must be generally intelligent.

CORRECTION:
Autonomy describes the degree of independent operation,
not the breadth of intelligence.


MISTAKE 6:
Assuming general-purpose AI automatically equals AGI.

CORRECTION:
Supporting many tasks does not establish broad and reliable
general intelligence.


MISTAKE 7:
Assuming all AI requires human supervision at every step.

CORRECTION:
Some systems operate autonomously within defined limits,
while others require human approval or continuous oversight.


MISTAKE 8:
Assuming limited memory means permanent learning.

CORRECTION:
A system can use historical context without changing
its model parameters.


MISTAKE 9:
Assuming Theory of Mind means emotion recognition alone.

CORRECTION:
Theory of Mind concerns modeling mental states such as
beliefs, knowledge, and intentions.


MISTAKE 10:
Assuming human review guarantees a correct outcome.

CORRECTION:
Human reviewers can also make mistakes. Oversight must
be supported by appropriate procedures and training.

2.22 CHAPTER SUMMARY
================================================================

1. AI CAN BE CLASSIFIED IN MULTIPLE WAYS.

   Classification by capability, functionality, purpose,
   and autonomy describes different dimensions.


2. ANI

   Specialized capability for particular tasks or a bounded
   collection of tasks.


3. AGI

   Proposed broad and flexible intelligence across domains.
   Its definition and achievement criteria remain contested.


4. ASI

   Hypothetical broad intellectual capabilities that
   substantially exceed human performance.


5. REACTIVE MACHINES

   Select actions primarily from the current situation
   without relying on stored observation history.


6. LIMITED MEMORY AI

   Uses relevant past observations, historical information,
   or internal state.


7. THEORY OF MIND AI

   A conceptual category involving the modeling of other
   agents' beliefs, intentions, knowledge, and mental states.


8. SELF-AWARE AI

   A hypothetical category involving genuine self-awareness
   or subjective experience.


9. WEAK AI

   Commonly refers to specialized AI without implying
   human-like general intelligence or consciousness.


10. STRONG AI

    May refer to general intelligence or to stronger
    philosophical claims about machine minds.


11. SPECIALIZED AI

    Focuses on a particular domain or task.


12. GENERAL-PURPOSE AI

    Supports a variety of tasks but is not automatically AGI.


13. AUTONOMOUS AI

    Selects or executes actions with some degree of independence.


14. HUMAN-IN-THE-LOOP AI

    Incorporates human participation into a workflow.


15. MODERN AI

    Includes ML, DL, foundation models, LLMs, multimodal
    systems, and tool-using applications.
